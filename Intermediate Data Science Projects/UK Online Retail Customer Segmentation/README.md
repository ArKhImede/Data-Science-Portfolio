# 🛒 UK Online Retail Customer Segmentation

Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA), Feature Engineering, Customer Analytics, and Unsupervised Machine Learning project using the UK Online Retail dataset. This project analyzes purchasing behavior, customer value, transaction patterns, and customer segmentation using K-Means clustering to identify meaningful customer groups and support business decision-making.

## 📂 Dataset

The dataset contains transaction-level data from a UK-based online retail company.

Features include:

• Invoice number

• Product identifier

• Product description

• Quantity purchased

• Invoice date

• Unit price

• Customer ID

• Country

The dataset covers online retail transactions across multiple countries, with the majority of sales originating from the United Kingdom.

This dataset is widely used for customer segmentation, purchasing behavior analysis, and unsupervised machine learning projects.

You can use this [link](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/shashanks1202/retail-transactions-online-sales-dataset) to download the dataset.

## 🔍 Key Insights

### 🆔 Anonymous customers spend more

Transactions without customer identifiers generated higher average revenue than transactions linked to identifiable customers.

### 📈 Revenue varies substantially throughout the year

November generated over £1.4 million in revenue, more than double several lower-performing months.

### 🌍 The business is heavily concentrated in the UK

Approximately 92% of transactions originated from the United Kingdom.

### 💰 Customer spending is highly skewed

A small number of customers account for extremely large purchases, creating a strong right-skew in total spending.

### 📦 Purchasing behavior varies considerably

Some customers purchase many unique products, while others repeatedly purchase a smaller set of products.

### 💸 More items do not always imply higher spending

Customers purchasing large quantities of inexpensive products may spend less than customers purchasing fewer premium products.

### 📈 Frequency strongly relates to spending

Customers who place more orders generally generate substantially higher total revenue.

### ⏰ Recency has a weak negative relationship with spending

More recently active customers tend to spend slightly more than customers who have not purchased recently.

## 🤖 Customer Segmentation

K-Means clustering was used to identify groups of customers with similar purchasing behavior.

### 🔧 Data Preparation

Before clustering:

• Log transformations were applied to highly skewed features

• Features were standardized using StandardScaler

### 📏 Cluster Selection

The optimal number of clusters was determined using:

• Elbow Method

• Silhouette Analysis

Three clusters were selected based on model performance and interpretability.

### 🎯 Clustering Features

The clustering model used:

• Recency

• Frequency

• Monetary

• Average Order Value

• Unique Products

• Items Per Order

### 📊 Cluster Visualization

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was used to reduce dimensionality and visualize customer segments.

## 🏆 Customer Segments

### 🔹 Cluster 0 — Occasional Customers

Approximately 2,112 customers.

Characteristics:

• Low spending

• Low purchase frequency

• Higher recency values

• Less recently active

These customers represent inactive or occasional buyers.

### 🔸 Cluster 1 — Core Customers

Approximately 2,182 customers.

Characteristics:

• Higher spending

• Higher purchase frequency

• Regular purchasing behavior

• Largest customer segment

This group represents the company's primary customer base.

Revenue contribution:

• Approximately 73.7% of total revenue

### ⭐ Cluster 2 — VIP Customers

Approximately 18 customers.

Characteristics:

• Extremely high purchase frequency

• Very high spending

• Large average order values

• Highly engaged customers

Despite representing less than 1% of customers, this segment contributes a disproportionately large share of total revenue.

Revenue contribution:

• Approximately 17.6% of total revenue

These customers likely represent wholesale buyers, business customers, or highly loyal VIP customers.
